# Scia — evidence

What was seen of each item of the [roadmap](./roadmap.md), how, and what was not. One section per item; the roadmap's tables link here. A line is moved here as the roadmap had it, so that a claim keeps its words. The owner's decisions behind each item are in [decisions.md](./decisions.md).

"Seen in the real app" means the built Electron app on Windows, driven by a script unless the line says a hand. "Held by specs" means no run of the app has met it.

## M0 — Foundations

### Monorepo and tooling

Monorepo, tooling, hooks, tests on four layers.

### The repository and its pipeline

Repository on GitHub with GitFlow, checks on PRs on Ubuntu and on Windows ([ADR 0008](../adr/0008-the-delivery-pipeline.md)) — from #90 on, the workflow runs them again on the commit a merge makes, before it is released — seen on the merge of #94, whose gates ran green on both systems before semantic-release, which found nothing to release; a red merged commit, which is to make no tag, has not happened yet — automatic versions and release notes (semantic-release).

The Windows installer came out of CI on request three times (`v1.0.0-beta.10` to `.12`); from `v1.0.0-beta.13` on, the workflow builds it for every release, beta or stable, with the files an updater reads (#83) — `.13`'s run itself dispatched by hand, because GitHub recorded no push for the merge of #86 (#95). Two built by CI were installed and run: `v1.0.0-beta.10`, and `v1.0.0-beta.12` on a scratch profile, its log read (#78). Both were installed silently from the command line (#78). Since #92 (#111), CI installs every release's installer silently on a runner before attaching it ([the installer in CI](#the-installer-in-ci)). None has been installed by hand from a download, where SmartScreen asks first, nor on a clean machine by a hand ([the installer by hand](#the-installer-by-hand)).

An installer run over an installed Scia was tried twice (#82), neither time by hand from a download: through the updater, from a local build of its branch to `v1.0.0-beta.13`'s installer, with the app run afterwards as `1.0.0-beta.13`; and from the command line, `v1.0.0-beta.13`'s installer with `--updated /S` — the updater's arguments — over `v1.0.0-beta.10`, with the installed entry, the folder and the uninstaller read, and the app not run. What was seen of the folder: a local build run over `v1.0.0-beta.10` stayed in its folder (#79), and so did that second try — an install of `v1.0.0-beta.10` or `.11` stays in `Programs\@sciadesktop` through every later installer, until it is uninstalled.

**The Linux packages have never been built** (`docs/development/release-flow.md`, "Not there yet").

### The engine

Downloads over several connections with dynamic segmentation, pause and resume, a queue, retries after an interruption, safe names that never overwrite, state saved on disk.

### The Electron app

A window with its own title bar, supervision of the engine, orderly quit, resume after a restart.

### The interface

Adding by paste, a list with the segment bar, filters, basic settings.

### The extension

"Download with Scia" menu, media detected in the page, optional hand-over of the browser's downloads (Chromium). **Written and covered by tests in its logic; not yet tried in a real browser.** Until it is published, Settings does not offer the code that pairs it: a code for something nobody can install (the owner, #152); the engine keeps it.

## M1 — Daily use

### The tray icon

Closing the window does not stop the downloads (#6). **Written, covered by specs, and verified by script in the real app on Windows** (the window hides, the download goes on, a second launch brings it back, closing with nothing left quits). **Not done until someone has seen it**: the icon on a taskbar, its click, its menu, its tooltip and the notification have never been looked at or pressed (#13). Linux: nothing of the tray has been tried there, and its tooltip cannot follow the downloads (#13).

The four decisions of the owner on #13 (21 November 2026), built in #256:

- **No tray icon, a close quits.** Where no tray could be made, closing the window quits Scia, also with downloads running, and they go on at the next start: held by a spec of the close (`close-policy.test.ts`) and by the E2E suite's restart. On a Linux desktop with no tray host the icon exists and shows nowhere, and Electron does not say so: not built, with #13's "Linux" list.
- **The window at once.** The window shows from its first moment on a page of main's own that says "Scia is starting…", in the theme the engine said last (kept by main in `theme.json`); a second launch, or the tray's "Open Scia", shows it too (read in the code; no spec starts Scia twice during a start). **Held** by the E2E suite, which runs in CI: the window visible before the engine is ready, its first page the starting one, and the theme chosen in Settings at the next start. **Not seen**: the page as drawn, a cold start after an install, a second launch during it (#13).
- **A shutdown or a sign-out.** Windows' end of the session (`session-end`, which a hidden window hears too) has the engine stop and write its state at once, installs nothing and starts nothing, and does not ask Windows to wait. Electron 44 ends the process itself the moment that handler returns unless its own quit has begun — a probe on CI read exit code 0 45 ms after the message, with no `will-quit` — so Scia begins it there and lets it stand until the engine has stopped (#262). **Held** by the E2E suite, which runs in CI, for a message after which nothing ends the process: a real `WM_QUERYENDSESSION` answered yes and a real `WM_ENDSESSION` (a shutdown, a sign-out) sent to the built app in the tray during a download — `state.json` with the download waiting and its bytes, Scia's own `quit` line, written once the engine's process had ended, and the download finished at the next start. **Not seen**: a real shutdown or sign-out, where Windows may end Scia, and the engine with it, as soon as every window has answered: whether the state is written in time then is not known, and the downloads go on from the bytes on disk either way (#13).
- **An installer run during a download.** The installer tells a Scia that runs from its folder that its session ends for it (`WM_ENDSESSION`, `ENDSESSION_CLOSEAPP`), keeps a handle on its process and waits for the process to end, fifteen seconds at most from the message, and only then runs electron-builder's own check, which ends what is left by force. It waits on the process, not the window: told so, Scia closes its window at once and ends once its engine has stopped. An installer waits, so the end of the session there is not cut short. **Held** by an integration spec of the include against a hidden window of Chromium's class whose process outlives it by three seconds — the include returns only once the process has ended — and by the installer's smoke test in CI: a repair run over the installed app during a download — Scia ended, with its state written, before the installer did, and finished the download at its next start. **Not seen**: an installer run by hand, and an update by hand from a version before #256, which knows nothing of Scia's handling of the message: Electron ends it at once, or electron-builder's check after the fifteen seconds (#13).

### The mark of a downloaded file

A file Scia downloads is marked as coming from the network — on Windows the zone only, never the address it came from — and a downloaded program asks for a second press before it is run (#22). **Written, covered by specs, and seen in the real app on Windows**: the stream is on the finished file; a double click and a key held down run nothing, they make the button ask; and after the second press Windows itself asks before a marked script is run ([ADR 0003](../adr/0003-the-mark-of-a-downloaded-file.md)). Not seen (#33): a marked `.exe`, installer or document (the files run were scripts); a disk without streams (held by a spec with a mark that refuses, never tried on a real one); a hand on the mouse and on the keys (the presses were sent by a script). Linux: no mark is written there.

### The speed limit

**For all downloads together, in Settings (#41): written, covered by specs, and seen in the real app on Windows** — a menu of ready values, with no number to type; a download held at the rate chosen, freed when the limit is lifted, held again at another rate without being stopped; three downloads held together under the one limit; against a host that sends as fast as it is read, counted in bytes: three downloads of eight connections at the lowest ready value, within one read of what the limit gives; ten of sixteen at that value, the same, each connection's turn 40 s apart — longer than the engine waits for a silent host — and no request made twice; a download under 25 and under 50 MB/s, the highest, at 99 and 100 % of the limit — its spec was in quarantine from #195 until #43 was fixed, and now measures the rate in the middle of the download, in bytes the engine took; a download paused in its stride leaving nothing owed to the next; files that arrive in one read held like the others; the limit kept after a restart and said beside the total speed, while there is a speed to show; a state file written before the limit existed loads.

Not seen (#43): a hand on the menu (a script set its value), the keys on it, and the list it opens, which the system draws, in either variant — but once, read from the system in the review of #52: its rows the theme's, its chosen row Chromium's own blue; Settings and the title bar with a limit in the light variant; the row of a download that waits its turn; HTTPS in the app (measured with scripts, not in it); a host that is not local read from this slowly, or going silent; Linux; the packaged app. The limit of one download: not built (#42).

### The notification when a download ends

A notification when a download ends (#45); "Open" and "Show in folder" are already there. Only while the window is not in front — read in review as "not the window in use"; one for each file, shared by those that end together; a click opens the window; no switch in Settings for now. For the last download of a Scia put away in the tray — completed or failed — it also says that nothing else is downloading and that Scia is still in the system tray, where it stays until it is quit from its menu.

**Written, covered by specs, and verified by script in the real app on Windows.** The script read main's log: how many downloads ended and how, where the window was, the title main handed the system, how many lines its body had and whether the tray's sentence was among them, and that the system answered "shown". By that: nothing is asked of the system while main takes the window for in front; a download that completes is notified with the window shown and not the one in use, and with it minimised; with it minimised five that end together share one notification, and one that fails is notified; so is the last download of a Scia put away in the tray, with the tray's sentence; it goes on after the engine is started again; and a Scia left idle for eleven minutes keeps hearing its engine.

**Held by specs only**: the names in the body, a Scia that was in use and is left by a click into another window, a failure as the last download of a hidden Scia. **Not done until someone has seen it**: nobody has looked at a notification or clicked one (#13). Linux: untried.

### The window really in use

Before #62 was built: a window that was shown while another was in use could believe it was in front, and Scia was then silent until the person clicks into it and away, or minimises it, or closes it into the tray — a start during which they went back to their work. Under script it did at some starts and not at others, and what decides it is not known; not seen by hand (#62).

**Built** (#62, [ADR 0020](../adr/0020-the-window-in-use-asked-of-windows.md)): on Windows main asks the system which window is in use (`GetForegroundWindow`, through koffi) when a notification is about to be decided; a system that does not answer counts as another window in use. **Seen**: unit specs of the decisions (the system believed over the window; no answer counted as another window in use); an integration spec of the real call (a handle, in microseconds); and the E2E suite in CI, where Windows answers inside the built app (`asked: 'answered'` on the clipboard's decisions). The E2E suite does not reach the case itself, a shown window while another is in use. **Not seen**: a start by hand, back to another window with no click into Scia, then a download that ends (#13).

### A second failure notified

When a download fails again within a quarter of a second of being tried again, while something else downloads, its second failure is not said to a person who leaves the window within two seconds (#55).

### Links taken from the clipboard

Clipboard watching (optional): a copied link is offered (#128). A switch in Settings, "Take links from the clipboard", off by default; the copied links that are files (not images, not web pages), file hosts' links or the page of a video on a site of a list kept in Scia are added as "Ready", not started, each with Start and Remove, and "Start all" while there are ready rows; a link already in the list is not added twice; ready rows are kept after a restart; a notification of the system says how many were added when the window is not in front ([ADR 0012](../adr/0012-links-taken-from-the-clipboard.md)).

**Written, covered by specs, and run in the real app on Windows by the E2E suite, with Electron's read of the clipboard answered by the spec** — links copied while the switch was on added ready, a page and an image left out, a link already there not added again, nothing fetched before "Start all", both files downloaded byte for byte after it, nothing taken while the switch was off nor what was on the clipboard when it was turned on again, the system asked for "Added 2 links, ready to start" with the window minimised, no link in the log.

**The start of the reading** (#134, the owner's decision; [ADR 0012](../adr/0012-links-taken-from-the-clipboard.md), its amendment): until it knows what was there, main asks the clipboard's formats before its text. **Written, covered by specs, and run in the real app on Windows by the E2E suite in CI, with Electron's text and formats answered by the spec** — with no text on the clipboard at the switch-on, a link copied as soon as main had looked was added "Ready"; with a link on a clipboard kept closed for five reads, longer than the three empty reads that used to call it empty, nothing was taken once it opened, and the next link was. The specs hold the rest: formats the system will not tell, an answer of a turn before an off and on, a text copied between the formats and the read. That Electron 44's `has('text/plain')` answers from the formats without opening the clipboard is read in its source, not seen.

**And tried by the owner's hand on Windows (21 November 2026), with the system's own clipboard** — on the PR's first head, before the review changed how the reading starts (what was there, refused and empty reads), which specs and the E2E suite hold — on a development build started by the owner (a sandboxed shell cannot open the clipboard) on a scratch profile, against local hosts and a simulated AllDebrid: the switch turned on in Settings; six lines copied at once from Notepad — two files of a local host, a file host's link, a YouTube video's page, a web page and an image — and the four expected added "Ready", the page and the image left out; the first line copied again added nothing; with Scia minimised, one more link copied, the notification "Added 1 link, ready to start" seen and clicked, and the window back; the YouTube row removed; "Start all" downloaded the three files byte for byte, and the file host's row said it needs a service. The notification wore Electron's logo, as a development build's does (#85).

Not seen: a file host's link through a connected service or a video's page started from a ready row, the Ready row and "Start all" in the eight themes that are not the default one, the start of the reading with the system's own clipboard — a picture on it, or a link on it while another program holds it open (#134) — the packaged app, Linux.

### The `--inspect` fuse off

The shipped exe ignores `--inspect` (#115): its fuse `enableNodeCliInspectArguments` is off in `apps/desktop/electron-builder.yml`, beside `runAsNode` and `enableNodeOptionsEnvironmentVariable`, so that whoever can start Scia cannot open a debugger on main with an argument. **Written (#115)**: the installer's smoke test starts the installed exe with `--inspect=0` among its arguments and reads on its stderr that Chromium opened its debugging port and that Node opened no debugger; the rest of the smoke test drives the exe by that port alone (`apps/desktop/e2e/installed.ts`). **Seen** on the PR's `Installer smoke (Windows)`, Gates run 45131149690: the eight specs green, the fuse's checks among them, after a first run in which playwright-core found no page (#237). Not tried by a hand. The shipped exe still honours Chromium's `--remote-debugging-port`, as the owner chose on #115: it reaches the page and, through it, the engine's API with the page's token — never main.

### Automatic updates

Automatic updates (#82). An installed Scia receives the stable releases, and the betas too when a switch in Settings, off by default, is on; it downloads an update by itself, in silence, only while it downloads nothing else, and installs it when the person quits it — never a restart while a download is running, and it stays closed afterwards (a restart the person asks for, from #133 on, opens it again: [the version and a downloaded update](#the-version-and-a-downloaded-update)); nothing is installed when the system ends Scia, at shutdown or sign-out; and every release is to carry its installer and what the updater reads, betas included ([ADR 0007](../adr/0007-automatic-updates.md)). Every release carries them since `v1.0.0-beta.13` (#83).

**Written, covered by specs, and seen in the installed app on Windows against this repository's releases** (#82): a beta with the switch off found nothing to take; with it on, it found `v1.0.0-beta.13`, held its download while a download of the person's ran and gave way to one added meanwhile, installed it at "Quit" in the tray and at a close with nothing left, and started again as `1.0.0-beta.13`; a quit while the update was being fetched installed nothing.

Not seen: a stable release taken (none exists); a quit by the system — Electron's documentation says Windows ends an app at a shutdown or a sign-out without the quit Scia installs on, so nothing is installed then: read, not seen; a beta waiting at the quit while its switch was turned off, and one found before the switch was turned off and then not fetched (held by specs); the check six hours later; Scia opened again during the seconds the installer runs; an installer cut off midway; Linux. Scia updates itself from the first release that carries this, the first beta after #82: an install of an earlier one is updated by hand once.

### Zero-filling on NTFS

#182; ADR 0001, "to be measured before M1 closes". Measured on 23 November 2026, on Windows 11 (10.0.26200), an i7-12700KF with 64 GB of memory, Node 26.13.1, Defender's real-time protection on, by two throwaway scripts (not committed):

- the engine's own `DownloadTask` and `HttpSource`, unchanged, fetching a file from a local host that generates its bytes, over 8 connections (the default) and 16 (the most), into a part file on the disk; every write timed, each connection's first write logged, the bytes written counted each second, a tiny `stat` of another file made every 50 ms to time the four threads Node gives the disk, and every byte compared afterwards (none wrong);
- `node:fs` alone: the part file made at its full size with `truncate`, then a write of 256 KB at the start of each of 8 ranges at once.

Each run is set against the same part file marked sparse (`fsutil sparse setflag`) before its size is set.

**Seen.** `truncate` itself takes milliseconds (13 ms at 16 GiB). A first write far into the file then waits while NTFS writes zeros up to it, and with 8 connections those waits hold all four of Node's disk threads: no byte of the download reaches the disk — the first connection's included — and every other disk operation of the engine (a save of `state.json`, the page's files, another download) waits as long.

| -------------------------- | ------ | ----------- | ---------------- | ------------------- | -------------- | -------------- | ------------- |
| NVMe SSD (Samsung 970 EVO) | 1 GiB  | 8           | 0.4 s            | —                   | 0.3 s          | 2.8 s          | 2.9 s         |
| NVMe SSD                   | 4 GiB  | 8           | 1.3 s            | —                   | 1.2 s          | 9.9 s          | 9.8 s         |
| NVMe SSD                   | 16 GiB | 8           | 12.1 s           | —                   | 11.0 s         | 38.4 s         | 29.6 s        |
| NVMe SSD                   | 32 GiB | 8           | 32.5 s, 33.2 s   | 32 s                | 32.4 s, 29.5 s | 81.1 s, 72.1 s | 47.2 s        |
| NVMe SSD                   | 32 GiB | 16          | 21.8 s           | 20 s                | 20.8 s         | 64.2 s         | 40.2 s        |
| USB hard disk (Seagate M3) | 4 GiB  | 8           | 65.6 s           | over 45 s           | 65.4 s         | 156.6 s        | 113.3 s       |

"Nothing written for" is read from the bytes counted each second, in the runs that counted them (the first 45 seconds). Two numbers are two runs. With the part file sparse, every connection wrote its first byte within 110 ms of the start on both disks, at every size, and no `stat` waited over 3 s. With `node:fs` alone the zeros came at about 1 GB/s on the SSD (a write at 28 GiB done after 29.6 s) and 60 MB/s on the USB disk (a write at 3.5 GiB done after 63 s).

A part file made at its full size takes its room on the disk at once (a 32 GiB file: 32 GiB less free), so that a full disk says so at the start; marked sparse, it takes none (0 GiB less free) until its bytes are written.

**The fix** ([ADR 0026](../adr/0026-a-part-file-made-sparse.md); the owner's decision on #182): a ranged part file is made sparse by the system's `fsutil` before its size is set, its room on the disk is looked at when it is made instead of reserved — less room than the file's size fails `DISK` before a byte is asked for — and the engine starts with 164 disk threads (`UV_THREADPOOL_SIZE`). **Held by specs**: a new part file asked to be made sparse while it is empty, and on Windows read back sparse with `fsutil sparse queryflag` (the task, and a download of the manager); a download whose part file cannot be made sparse finished and logged `part_file_not_sparse`, a pair's two files each; a file larger than the room left refused before its host is asked, with no part file made — also one whose kept part file is gone; a kept part file, dense at its full size, taken up again whatever room is left, its room not looked at again; a disk that does not say its room not checked; what the marker runs — `fsutil` by its full path, its arguments, an environment of `SystemRoot` alone, hidden, its limit — and a program that never answers ended at the limit, the download finished and the refusal logged; main giving the engine the threads. Each line of the fix was taken away, or turned, one at a time, and a spec failed for each. **Held by CI's E2E suite on the built app**: a download paused, its `.scia` part file read back sparse with `fsutil sparse queryflag`, and no `part_file_not_sparse` in the log.

**Measured again with the fix**, the same day, machine and script, the 32 GiB file on the NVMe SSD over 8 connections, with the engine's own marker and `UV_THREADPOOL_SIZE=164`; and the part file dense with the 164 threads alone:

| Run                    | Last first write | Nothing written for | Longest `stat` | First second | Whole          |
| ---------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------- | -------------- | ------------ | -------------- |
| Sparse, 164 threads    | 61 ms            | —                   | 10 ms          | 0.32 GiB     | 49.2 s         |
| Dense, 164 threads     | 27.1 s           | 26 s                | 13 ms          | 0            | 68.0 s         |
| Before (dense, 4), × 2 | 32.5 s, 33.2 s   | 32 s                | 32.4 s, 29.5 s | 0            | 81.1 s, 72.1 s |

The first writes' 61 ms include `fsutil` and the look at the room. `fsutil sparse setflag` took 22 to 39 ms (median 25, of 20); the 164 threads took 9 MiB more of a Node process (76 to 85 MiB). The threads alone keep the engine's other disk work from waiting; the download still waits for the zeros.

**Not seen** (#265 carries the first three): a large file in the app, by a person; the USB hard disk with the fix; a download refused for room in the app; `UV_THREADPOOL_SIZE` reaching the engine's `utilityProcess`; a network slower than the disk, where the zeros are written while the connections would otherwise wait on the host; a disk with Defender's real-time protection off; a full disk met by a download whose room was there when its part file was made; a finished file left sparse, and what other programs make of it; Linux.

Connect AllDebrid in Settings, then try again." instead of saving the host's page as the file ([ADR 0006](../adr/0006-a-file-host-without-a-service.md)). **Written, covered by specs, and seen in the real app on Windows against a simulated service** — with no service connected, a file host's link said so on its row and nothing was written to the folder; a direct link pasted beside it downloaded as before; the one request the service received was the public list, with no key; once connected, "Retry" unlocked the link and the file arrived byte for byte, compared with what its host sent (`docs/design/needs-a-service.png`). AllDebrid's real public list was read by a plain request outside the app, not by the app.

Not seen: the real list in the app; a list that cannot be read while a link is added, one read again after six hours, a service that never answers, and a key connected while the list is read (specs only); Settings, Connect and Retry by keys (pressed with the pointer); a hand on the keys; Linux; the packaged app.

### Connected by a code

AllDebrid connected by a code confirmed on its site (#136), beside the pasted key: "Connect with a code" is the main way in Settings, and pasting a key stays as the other. AllDebrid's PIN flow: Scia shows a code, opens AllDebrid's page where the person confirms it, and receives the key, kept as a pasted one is ([ADR 0004](../adr/0004-the-key-of-a-service.md), which had named this flow "not now"; [ADR 0013](../adr/0013-a-key-that-arrives-by-a-code.md)). Built first, before magnets.

**Written, covered by specs, and seen in the real app on Windows against a simulated service** — "Connect with a code", given the focus by the harness and pressed with Enter, showed the code, `alldebrid.com/pin` and "works for 10 min", and gave the focus to "Open AllDebrid"; its press had main ask the engine and call the system to open `https://alldebrid.com/pin/?pin=<code>`, the one page asked of the service having carried no key; the code confirmed at the simulated service connected Scia 4.9 s later — the engine asks every five seconds, the page every two, with the account's name, and the focus on "Disconnect"; the key sealed in the profile's `keys/`, in no log and not in `state.json` (`docs/design/connect-with-a-code.png`).

Not seen: AllDebrid's real page and a real account, which are the owner's to try; the person's browser opened by the system (the run wrote down what main asked it to open); a code that expires, that AllDebrid refuses, or whose key the system will not keep, Cancel, and Settings closed while a code waits or is still coming (specs only); a code whose page the person leaves unconfirmed when Settings closes is forgotten, and confirming it afterwards connects nothing — said nowhere on screen (#146); a hand on the keys; Linux; the packaged app. The routes each new piece adds, in Scia's client and in its simulated service, have to be checked against AllDebrid's documentation, not against the older OpenAPI specification its tools page offers (#136).

### A pasted magnet

A magnet pasted into Scia is added to AllDebrid at once, and its files, once AllDebrid has them, download by themselves in a folder named after the magnet, its sub-folders rebuilt as the magnet has them — as JDownloader rebuilds them from what AllDebrid gives (#137). **Written, covered by specs, and seen in the real app on Windows against a simulated service** ([ADR 0014](../adr/0014-magnets-through-a-service.md)) — a magnet typed in the field and sent with Enter showed its row at once, "Sending to AllDebrid…", then "AllDebrid is fetching it · 50% · 12.0 MB of 24.0 MB · 1.0 MB/s" on a bar of one stretch (`docs/design/a-magnet.png`); once ready it left the list, and its two files arrived byte for byte in `Holiday films` and `Holiday films/Extras`, unlocked through the service; the simulated service, which the magnet's files reached after four questions, counted five questions of where the magnet was, and one each of the upload and of the files; neither the magnet nor its name in the log. With no service connected, a magnet typed in the field showed at once why it failed and where to connect AllDebrid (`docs/design/a-magnet-without-a-service.png`); Retry, pressed with Enter, said it again and left the focus on Remove; Remove took the row away; the service heard nothing. Scia reads the tree of `/v4/magnet/files`, not JDownloader's `getMagnet` page (ADR 0014, point 4).

Not seen: the real AllDebrid — that its `alldebrid.com/f/…` links unlock as a hoster's do, and how it lists a file it packed into an archive — which are the owner's to try; a magnet pasted with Ctrl+V on the page (typed in the field: the sandboxed shell cannot open the clipboard); a magnet the service refuses or fails, an account not premium, a restart while one waits (specs only); a hand on the keys; Linux; the packaged app.

### The magnets of the account

The magnets of the account — also those added on AllDebrid's site or by other applications — in a side panel that folds away, there only while AllDebrid is connected and closed by default; the list of downloads stays the one main screen (#138). **Written, covered by specs, and seen in the real app on Windows against a simulated service** ([ADR 0016](../adr/0016-the-magnets-of-the-account.md)) — no button for the panel before AllDebrid was connected, one after, the panel closed; opened with Enter, the focus on its heading, it listed the account's three magnets, one failed, one fetching and one ready, added elsewhere (`docs/design/the-account-magnets.png`); its Download, pressed twice with Enter, took the ready one once — "In Scia’s list" — and its file arrived byte for byte in its folder, nothing sent to AllDebrid; Escape closed it, the focus back on its button; with no magnet of Scia's waiting, so that the panel alone asked, the simulated service counted two questions about the account in an 11-second window begun about three seconds after the panel opened, and none in 11 seconds once it closed; no magnet's name in the log. Started by Electron itself, the window minimised and then hidden with the panel open: the page was `hidden` and the account asked nothing for 12 seconds, then asked once within 2 seconds of being shown. At the window's narrowest, 640 by 720, the panel stayed beside the list, which narrowed with nothing scrolling sideways and every button whole (`docs/design/the-account-magnets-narrow.png`).

Not seen: the real AllDebrid — the order and the dates it lists magnets with, an account with many magnets, a refused key — which are the owner's to try; the eight themes other than the default (their colours by the contrast spec only); a hand on the keys; Linux; the packaged app.

Its E2E spec runs in CI only, at the owner's request, with the clipboard answered in main.

Not seen: the real clipboard, and the real AllDebrid — a copied magnet it refuses at the upload among it — the owner's to try; the real app on this machine (no run that opens Electron's windows, at the owner's request); Linux; the packaged app.
