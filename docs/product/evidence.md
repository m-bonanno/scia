# Scia — evidence

What was seen of each item of the [roadmap](./roadmap.md), how, and what was not. One section per item; the roadmap's tables link here. A line is moved here as the roadmap had it, so that a claim keeps its words. The owner's decisions behind each item are in [decisions.md](./decisions.md).

"Seen in the real app" means the built Electron app on Windows, driven by a script unless the line says a hand. "Held by specs" means no run of the app has met it. A Beta report — an issue opened with its form — is read into the item it tried, as a line that names the beta, the system and the issue, and what it saw in its own words. One from someone outside the team that answers "Yes, as I expected" to the form's "Did it work?" (`.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/beta.yml`) proves the item as _Report_ in the roadmap's **Proven by**, the word linked to its issue: evidence of its own, beside what the owner's hand is to see, never in its place (the owner, 24 November 2026, #327). It does not make an item done alone — an item needs the owner's _Hand_ or a _Script_ to be ticked, but for the lines of the gate the owner ticked on other proof (the roadmap's key, 25 November 2026, #339) — and a _Script_ is the stronger proof of the two, since it runs again on the app built from the repository. One that answers "Partly" or "No" is read in as what it saw, and proves nothing; a "No" for an item the roadmap already ticks opens a bug with the report's evidence, and the item stays ticked until the bug confirms it is broken.

## M0 — Foundations

### Monorepo and tooling

Monorepo, tooling, hooks, tests on four layers.

### The repository and its pipeline

Repository on GitHub with GitFlow, checks on PRs on Ubuntu and on Windows ([ADR 0008](../adr/0008-the-delivery-pipeline.md)) — from #90 on, the workflow runs them again on the commit a merge makes, before it is released — seen on the merge of #94, whose gates ran green on both systems before semantic-release, which found nothing to release; a red merged commit, which is to make no tag, has not happened yet — automatic versions and release notes (semantic-release).

The Windows installer came out of CI on request three times (`v1.0.0-beta.10` to `.12`); from `v1.0.0-beta.13` on, the workflow builds it for every release, beta or stable, with the files an updater reads (#83) — `.13`'s run itself dispatched by hand, because GitHub recorded no push for the merge of #86 (#95). Two built by CI were installed and run: `v1.0.0-beta.10`, and `v1.0.0-beta.12` on a scratch profile, its log read (#78). Both were installed silently from the command line (#78). Since #92 (#111), CI installs every release's installer silently on a runner before attaching it ([the installer in CI](#the-installer-in-ci)). None has been installed by hand from a download, where SmartScreen asks first, nor on a clean machine by a hand ([the installer by hand](#the-installer-by-hand)).

An installer run over an installed Scia was tried twice (#82), neither time by hand from a download: through the updater, from a local build of its branch to `v1.0.0-beta.13`'s installer, with the app run afterwards as `1.0.0-beta.13`; and from the command line, `v1.0.0-beta.13`'s installer with `--updated /S` — the updater's arguments — over `v1.0.0-beta.10`, with the installed entry, the folder and the uninstaller read, and the app not run. What was seen of the folder: a local build run over `v1.0.0-beta.10` stayed in its folder (#79), and so did that second try — an install of `v1.0.0-beta.10` or `.11` stays in `Programs\@sciadesktop` through every later installer, until it is uninstalled.

**The Linux packages have never been built** (`docs/development/release-flow.md`, "Not there yet").

### The engine

Downloads over several connections with dynamic segmentation, pause and resume, a queue, retries after an interruption, safe names that never overwrite, state saved on disk.

### The Electron app

A window with its own title bar, supervision of the engine, orderly quit, resume after a restart.

### The interface

Adding by paste, a list with the segment bar, filters, basic settings.

### The extension

"Download with Scia" menu, media detected in the page, optional hand-over of the browser's downloads (Chromium). **Written and covered by tests in its logic; not yet tried in a real browser.** Until it is published, Settings does not offer the code that pairs it: a code for something nobody can install (the owner, #152); the engine keeps it.

## M1 — Daily use

### The tray icon

Closing the window does not stop the downloads (#6). **Written, covered by specs, and verified by script in the real app on Windows** (the window hides, the download goes on, a second launch brings it back, closing with nothing left quits). **Not done until someone has seen it**: the icon on a taskbar, its click, its menu, its tooltip and the notification have never been looked at or pressed (#13). Linux: nothing of the tray has been tried there, and its tooltip cannot follow the downloads (#13).

The four decisions of the owner on #13 (21 November 2026), built in #256:

- **No tray icon, a close quits.** Where no tray could be made, closing the window quits Scia, also with downloads running, and they go on at the next start: held by a spec of the close (`close-policy.test.ts`) and by the E2E suite's restart. On a Linux desktop with no tray host the icon exists and shows nowhere, and Electron does not say so: not built, with #13's "Linux" list.
- **The window at once.** The window shows from its first moment on a page of main's own that says "Scia is starting…", in the theme the engine said last (kept by main in `theme.json`); a second launch, or the tray's "Open Scia", shows it too (read in the code; no spec starts Scia twice during a start). **Held** by the E2E suite, which runs in CI: the window visible before the engine is ready, its first page the starting one, and the theme chosen in Settings at the next start. **Not seen**: the page as drawn, a cold start after an install, a second launch during it (#13).
- **A shutdown or a sign-out.** Windows' end of the session (`session-end`, which a hidden window hears too) has the engine stop and write its state at once, installs nothing and starts nothing, and does not ask Windows to wait. Electron 44 ends the process itself the moment that handler returns unless its own quit has begun — a probe on CI read exit code 0 45 ms after the message, with no `will-quit` — so Scia begins it there and lets it stand until the engine has stopped (#262). **Held** by the E2E suite, which runs in CI, for a message after which nothing ends the process: a real `WM_QUERYENDSESSION` answered yes and a real `WM_ENDSESSION` (a shutdown, a sign-out) sent to the built app in the tray during a download — `state.json` with the download waiting and its bytes, the engine's `stopped` and then Scia's own `quit` line, written once the engine's process had ended and its output was read (#273), and the download finished at the next start. **Not seen**: a real shutdown or sign-out, where Windows may end Scia, and the engine with it, as soon as every window has answered: whether the state is written in time then is not known, and the downloads go on from the bytes on disk either way (#13).
- **An installer run during a download.** The installer tells a Scia that runs from its folder that its session ends for it (`WM_ENDSESSION`, `ENDSESSION_CLOSEAPP`), keeps a handle on its process and waits for the process to end, fifteen seconds at most from the message, and only then runs electron-builder's own check, which ends what is left by force. It waits on the process, not the window: told so, Scia closes its window at once and ends once its engine has stopped. An installer waits, so the end of the session there is not cut short. **Held** by an integration spec of the include against a hidden window of Chromium's class whose process outlives it by three seconds — the include returns only once the process has ended — and by the installer's smoke test in CI: a repair run over the installed app during a download — Scia ended, with its state written, before the installer did, and finished the download at its next start. **Not seen**: an installer run by hand, and an update by hand from a version before #256, which knows nothing of Scia's handling of the message: Electron ends it at once, or electron-builder's check after the fifteen seconds (#13).

### The notification when a download ends

### One write of `state.json` for the changes that come together

### `state.json` and a power cut

#347, found on the review of #346. A power cut keeps only what was synced, and the state could claim more by two roads:

- **A checkpoint read a download's progress after it synced the part file.** The connections write on during the sync, so the read counted bytes the sync had not covered.
- **A run that ended early closed its part file without a sync, and its progress was kept.** That is a pause, a failure or a quit.

Now a checkpoint reads first, then syncs, then keeps what it read, and keeps nothing for a run that ended or took a new task during its sync. A run that ends early syncs its part file before it lets it go.

**Seen**: specs on every system.

- **A slowed sync, at a checkpoint and at a pause.** Every sync is made 150 ms slower while the connections write on. As the store makes each state text, the bytes it claims are read back from the part file as it was when the newest sync that has ended began. This runs at every checkpoint, then at a pause and the quit.
- **A run that ends early.** A stopped run and a failed one each sync their part file on its own handle, while it is still open.
- **A download that completes during a checkpoint's sync.** Only the checkpoint's sync is slowed, by 300 ms. The download keeps all its bytes, in the engine and in the state.

Each was seen failing on macOS:

- the first on `develop`'s engine, on a checkpoint that reads after its sync, on a run that ends early without a sync, and on a run that lets the part file go before its sync;
- the second on `develop`'s task;
- the third without the checkpoint's check of the run: the completed download kept 0 bytes.

**Not seen**: a real power cut. The gate line of `v1.0.0` is ticked on its specs, by the owner's decision (25 November 2026, #347): it is not part of the owner's sitting. The half of the checkpoint's check that names a new task — taken when a service's link expires and another is asked for (#180) — is held by no spec: the road needs a link that expires during a checkpoint's sync.

### Zero-filling on NTFS

#182; ADR 0001, "to be measured before M1 closes". Measured on 23 November 2026, on Windows 11 (10.0.26200), an i7-12700KF with 64 GB of memory, Node 26.13.1, Defender's real-time protection on, by two throwaway scripts (not committed):

- the engine's own `DownloadTask` and `HttpSource`, unchanged, fetching a file from a local host that generates its bytes, over 8 connections (the default) and 16 (the most), into a part file on the disk; every write timed, each connection's first write logged, the bytes written counted each second, a tiny `stat` of another file made every 50 ms to time the four threads Node gives the disk, and every byte compared afterwards (none wrong);
- `node:fs` alone: the part file made at its full size with `truncate`, then a write of 256 KB at the start of each of 8 ranges at once.

Each run is set against the same part file marked sparse (`fsutil sparse setflag`) before its size is set.

**Seen.** `truncate` itself takes milliseconds (13 ms at 16 GiB). A first write far into the file then waits while NTFS writes zeros up to it, and with 8 connections those waits hold all four of Node's disk threads: no byte of the download reaches the disk — the first connection's included — and every other disk operation of the engine (a save of `state.json`, the page's files, another download) waits as long.

| Disk                       | File   | Connections | Last first write | Nothing written for | Longest `stat` | Whole, dense   | Whole, sparse |
| -------------------------- | ------ | ----------- | ---------------- | ------------------- | -------------- | -------------- | ------------- |
| NVMe SSD (Samsung 970 EVO) | 1 GiB  | 8           | 0.4 s            | —                   | 0.3 s          | 2.8 s          | 2.9 s         |
| NVMe SSD                   | 4 GiB  | 8           | 1.3 s            | —                   | 1.2 s          | 9.9 s          | 9.8 s         |
| NVMe SSD                   | 16 GiB | 8           | 12.1 s           | —                   | 11.0 s         | 38.4 s         | 29.6 s        |
| NVMe SSD                   | 32 GiB | 8           | 32.5 s, 33.2 s   | 32 s                | 32.4 s, 29.5 s | 81.1 s, 72.1 s | 47.2 s        |
| NVMe SSD                   | 32 GiB | 16          | 21.8 s           | 20 s                | 20.8 s         | 64.2 s         | 40.2 s        |
| USB hard disk (Seagate M3) | 4 GiB  | 8           | 65.6 s           | over 45 s           | 65.4 s         | 156.6 s        | 113.3 s       |

"Nothing written for" is read from the bytes counted each second, in the runs that counted them (the first 45 seconds). Two numbers are two runs. With the part file sparse, every connection wrote its first byte within 110 ms of the start on both disks, at every size, and no `stat` waited over 3 s. With `node:fs` alone the zeros came at about 1 GB/s on the SSD (a write at 28 GiB done after 29.6 s) and 60 MB/s on the USB disk (a write at 3.5 GiB done after 63 s).

A part file made at its full size takes its room on the disk at once (a 32 GiB file: 32 GiB less free), so that a full disk says so at the start; marked sparse, it takes none (0 GiB less free) until its bytes are written.

**The fix** ([ADR 0026](../adr/0026-a-part-file-made-sparse.md); the owner's decision on #182): a ranged part file is made sparse by the system's `fsutil` before its size is set, its room on the disk is looked at when it is made instead of reserved — less room than the file's size fails `DISK` before a byte is asked for — and the engine starts with 164 disk threads (`UV_THREADPOOL_SIZE`). **Held by specs**: a new part file asked to be made sparse while it is empty, and on Windows read back sparse with `fsutil sparse queryflag` (the task, and a download of the manager); a download whose part file cannot be made sparse finished and logged `part_file_not_sparse`, a pair's two files each; a file larger than the room left refused before its host is asked, with no part file made — also one whose kept part file is gone; a kept part file, dense at its full size, taken up again whatever room is left, its room not looked at again; a disk that does not say its room not checked; what the marker runs — `fsutil` by its full path, its arguments, an environment of `SystemRoot` alone, hidden, its limit — and a program that never answers ended at the limit, the download finished and the refusal logged; main giving the engine the threads. Each line of the fix was taken away, or turned, one at a time, and a spec failed for each. **Held by CI's E2E suite on the built app**: a download paused, its `.scia` part file read back sparse with `fsutil sparse queryflag`, and no `part_file_not_sparse` in the log.

**Measured again with the fix**, the same day, machine and script, the 32 GiB file on the NVMe SSD over 8 connections, with the engine's own marker and `UV_THREADPOOL_SIZE=164`; and the part file dense with the 164 threads alone:

| Run                    | Last first write | Nothing written for | Longest `stat` | First second | Whole          |
| ---------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------- | -------------- | ------------ | -------------- |
| Sparse, 164 threads    | 61 ms            | —                   | 10 ms          | 0.32 GiB     | 49.2 s         |
| Dense, 164 threads     | 27.1 s           | 26 s                | 13 ms          | 0            | 68.0 s         |
| Before (dense, 4), × 2 | 32.5 s, 33.2 s   | 32 s                | 32.4 s, 29.5 s | 0            | 81.1 s, 72.1 s |

The first writes' 61 ms include `fsutil` and the look at the room. `fsutil sparse setflag` took 22 to 39 ms (median 25, of 20); the 164 threads took 9 MiB more of a Node process (76 to 85 MiB). The threads alone keep the engine's other disk work from waiting; the download still waits for the zeros.

**Not seen** (#265 carries the first three): a large file in the app, by a person; the USB hard disk with the fix; a download refused for room in the app; `UV_THREADPOOL_SIZE` reaching the engine's `utilityProcess`; a network slower than the disk, where the zeros are written while the connections would otherwise wait on the host; a disk with Defender's real-time protection off; a full disk met by a download whose room was there when its part file was made; a finished file left sparse, and what other programs make of it; Linux.

## M3 — Integrations: debrid services

M3 does not wait for M2: a hoster link pasted into Scia needs nothing from it. Only the link sent by the extension leans on an extension that has not been tried in a browser yet.

Integrations arrive one at a time. Each is visible in three places: an **Integrations** section in Settings that lists the services and shows which are connected; its section of [integrations.md](./integrations.md), with its ADRs, issues and state, which the README's Integrations points to (a table in the README until #275); the release notes of the version that brings it (`feat(integrations): …`).

AllDebrid (#7), connected once in Settings (API key kept by the operating system). From then on a hoster link pasted into Scia, or sent by the extension, is recognised, unlocked and downloaded automatically, with nothing else to do — as a direct link would be. It has to be built and proven against a simulated service; the verification with a real account is the owner's, who pastes their own key into Settings. It is built in small PRs, each with its issue. Of #7's nine points, six are ticked by merged PRs (the audit of #7, 21 November 2026); #180 and #181 are built and held by specs, not yet seen in the app ([a link that expires](#a-link-that-expires), [a link into several files](#a-link-into-several-files)); with both landed, the README and ADR point is ticked: the README's row (since #275, AllDebrid's section of [integrations.md](./integrations.md)), the roadmap, and ADR 0005 with ADR 0025 beside it.

The first stable release waits for every line of M3 but a second service — the connection by a code, magnets and their follow-ups (#146, #150) included ([decisions.md](./decisions.md#21-november-2026)). When they were planned it did not wait for the code and magnets: the lines it waited for were what #7 lists under "Done when", which has more that has no line here. ### The magnets of the account

The magnets of the account — also those added on AllDebrid's site or by other applications — in a side panel that folds away, there only while AllDebrid is connected and closed by default; the list of downloads stays the one main screen (#138). **Written, covered by specs, and seen in the real app on Windows against a simulated service** ([ADR 0016](../adr/0016-the-magnets-of-the-account.md)) — no button for the panel before AllDebrid was connected, one after, the panel closed; opened with Enter, the focus on its heading, it listed the account's three magnets, one failed, one fetching and one ready, added elsewhere (`docs/design/the-account-magnets.png`); its Download, pressed twice with Enter, took the ready one once — "In Scia’s list" — and its file arrived byte for byte in its folder, nothing sent to AllDebrid; Escape closed it, the focus back on its button; with no magnet of Scia's waiting, so that the panel alone asked, the simulated service counted two questions about the account in an 11-second window begun about three seconds after the panel opened, and none in 11 seconds once it closed; no magnet's name in the log. Started by Electron itself, the window minimised and then hidden with the panel open: the page was `hidden` and the account asked nothing for 12 seconds, then asked once within 2 seconds of being shown. At the window's narrowest, 640 by 720, the panel stayed beside the list, which narrowed with nothing scrolling sideways and every button whole (`docs/design/the-account-magnets-narrow.png`).

### A magnet from the clipboard

A magnet copied to the clipboard, while "Take links from the clipboard" is on, is added to AllDebrid at once as a pasted one is — not left "Ready" as other copied links; a magnet copied by mistake holds one of AllDebrid's 30 active places until it is removed (#139, after #128). **Written and covered by specs** ([ADR 0017](../adr/0017-a-magnet-taken-from-the-clipboard.md)); with no service connected, a copied magnet is left out. Its E2E spec runs in CI only, at the owner's request, with the clipboard answered in main.
