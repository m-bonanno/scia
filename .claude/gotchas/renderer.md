# Gotchas — the renderer

For a change to `apps/renderer/` or `apps/extension/`: numbers, focus, keys, the pointer, what is drawn. Lint holds raw colours, `dark:` and `toFixed` in `apps/renderer/src`, its specs aside, and not in the extension (`eslint.config.js`). Read with the general entries of [`.claude/gotchas.md`](../gotchas.md); each entry's story is in the issues it names.

- → Grouping comes from the locale's CLDR data, not from intuition: `en-US` writes `1,000`, `it-IT` leaves `1000`. A spec expects what `Intl` says for the `LOCALE` of `@scia/contracts/locale`.
- → The unit is tied to its number with a no-break space, written as the escape `\u00a0`, never as the invisible character itself.
- → Testing Library normalises the white space of the text it reads, the no-break space included, but not of the string it is asked for: a spec finds a formatted amount with a regular expression (`/^10\.0\sMB\/s$/`).
- → A live region that is `display: none` while empty is outside the accessibility tree when its text arrives, and the announcement is often lost (#138). Keep the region drawn (`empty:py-0`), and check it where styles exist: jsdom applies no CSS, the E2E suite reads `getComputedStyle`.
- → A key held down repeats onto whatever the first press gives the focus to: a held Enter sent links, closed Settings, opened seven pages and restarted Scia (#135, #136, #157, #138). Arm the guard from the whole of what moves the focus — the row, the button and its popover — swallow the repeats of Enter alone until it is let go, and give the first focus to the least consequential answer.
- → What a component that stays mounted remembers is still there when it is shown again: a refusal said in one visit of Settings greeted the next (#38). Clear it when it opens, with a spec that closes and opens.
- → `userEvent.dblClick` on a button its first press replaces sends the second press to an element that is gone, so the spec passes whatever the code does (#32, #131). Fire what a browser sends (`fireEvent.click(button, { detail: 2 })`) — a harness's `clickCount: 2` over DevTools proves what the page does with a second press, not that a hand makes one — and make a control whose press changes what it does one element whose label follows the state (`MainAction`, `useOnePress`).
- → A guard written for the pointer is tried on the keyboard's twin: a held key repeats the press, as clicks whose `detail` is 0 (#32). The spec is `userEvent.keyboard('{Enter>5/}')`; the guard is `event.repeat`, for the key that presses and no other, so that a held Tab still walks on.
- → Chromium keeps a focus Tab moves out of an area's scroll padding, and leaves one the page moves (`element.focus()`, Playwright's `locator.focus()`) where it is once it is in the scrollport (#244). A spec of where a focus lands puts its target where the guard matters — in the scrollport, under what covers it — and takes both roads.
- → Room bought with padding grows what is sized by its content: a centred dialog grew by the notices' height and moved under the pointer that had just pressed in it (#244). Buy room only where the cover is, and measure that what is not covered does not move.
- → A handler on every focus runs for the focus a pointer gives at its press, and a scroll before `mouseup` lost the click (#244). Try a guard on focus with `page.mouse.click`, and leave the pointer's own focus alone (`pointerdown` in the same turn).
- → A mouse, a pen and a finger do not send their events in one order: a tap's `mousedown`, which gives the focus, comes a task or more after its `pointerdown`, and a guard marked at `pointerdown` lost 13 taps of 15 (#244). Mark a press with the event in the task of what it guards (`mousedown` for the focus), and try a guard of "the pointer" with a tap too (`Input.dispatchTouchEvent`).
- → `getComputedStyle(element).maxHeight` of a `calc()` is a string, not pixels, and a `parseFloat` fallback hid a branch the app never took (#244). A size the code computes with is the page's own constant, set on the element from that constant, or the used size (`getBoundingClientRect`).
- → A panel that lies over the page at a narrow width leaves focusable controls under it, and a flex item without `min-w-0` kept the list as wide as its widest line (#138). Try every width the window allows, down to its minimum, and measure the overflow, not the look.
- → A control that changes size or place when pressed is tried with the pointer where it will be: "Run", wider than "Open", took the next click of a burst, and a question wider than its word sent a button to the next line (#32, #302). A word that asks is as wide as its question from the start (`ConfirmButton`'s `shape: 'word'`).
- → A way back is not offered beside something that cannot be taken back: Cancel stayed pressable while a key was checked (#53), and a code kept coming after Cancel (#136). Try every control of a form while its request is on its way; every ask carries its turn, and an answer to one that is no longer the last is dropped and told to forget what it brought.
- → Whether to hand on a focus is decided when the control leaves, from where the focus is then, never from a flag set at the press (#131, #138). Remember the node that held it, and hand on only when that node is gone and the focus has gone nowhere since.
- → A handle given by `useImperativeHandle` with no list of dependencies is set to `null` and made again at every drawing, so a hand-on read in the same commit found it `null` (#302). Give a handle others read its dependencies (`[]` when it reads only refs), and try a hand-on from every shape of the tree it leaves from.
- → Chromium does not clip a `::-webkit-scrollbar` to its area's `border-radius`: the thumb of the rounded Settings ran onto the veil at its corners (#154). A rounded area that scrolls gives its track a `margin-block` of its radius, and a spec reads the corner's pixel beside the veil's.
